### Risk taxonomy

- The proposed taxonomy  groups different types of AI outputs according to their disclosure risks.
- The intention is effectively to provide a lookup process:
  1. Identify the type of output or model.
  2. Assign it to the relevant category.
  3. Identify the associated risks.
  4. Identify appropriate checks.
  5. Identify possible mitigations.
- The meaning of the phrase **“white hacker attack”** needs clarification.

### Consent and LoRA

- A potential consent issue was identified around low-rank adapters (LoRA).
- Unlike training only a new final layer, adapters can contain information derived from the training data at multiple layers.
- This could potentially allow somebody to repurpose a trained model by attaching a different head.
- That raises questions about whether information derived from sensitive data could subsequently be used for purposes outside the original consent or ethical approval.
- This needs further exploration to establish the practical implications for TRE disclosure control.

- Care is needed when reporting true-positive rates at very low false-positive rates.

## Attack metrics and reporting

- Several alternative approaches to reporting membership-inference risk were discussed:
  - sensitivity and specificity-based measures;
  - Youden’s index;
  - Bayes factors;
  - improvement over random guessing;
  - confidence intervals.
- No single metric is likely to capture every aspect of the problem.
- A possible approach is therefore to report multiple metrics while drawing attention to a smaller number of particularly useful ones.
- There is also a need to explain these measures in language that output checkers and other non-specialists can understand.
- Aggregate metrics may miss cases where several different attacks repeatedly identify the same records.
- Jim will consider discussing whether bootstrapping could be used to produce confidence intervals for LiRA.
